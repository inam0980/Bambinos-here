Executive summary

The pipeline is waiting on the wrong signal

Today a renewal moves only when a teacher's form arrives and an Ops agent checks it by hand. But the payment gateway already knows who paid, how much, and for which student, class and teacher. Steps 2 and 3 copy that information again, adding typos and delay. For a child continuing in the same batch, they add nothing.

Five things the data says

1
The queue is where renewals die, not the teacher. Teachers filed 10 of 12 forms within 50 minutes of payment. Once filed, 8 forms waited 16–48h, and the queue was not worked first-in-first-out: form 3303 (clean, filed Mon 09:30) is still pending, while 3305 and 3312, filed later, were approved.
2
"Approved" is not the finish line. Of 4 approved forms, only 1 child has classes booked. One booking failed as "batch full", but the child is one of the 6 students in that full batch. Another was approved for a weekend slot that does not exist.
3
Re-typing creates the errors that Ops then checks for. 25% of forms contain a duplicate, a wrong amount or a wrong invoice ID (84931 typed for 84913). One form was raised on a failed payment with only a UPI "processing" screenshot as proof.
4
Contact-based matching breaks on real families. A mother paying from her own phone and email, NRI numbers (+971, +1), and a parent name containing Cyrillic letters that look identical to Latin ones all break exact matching. The invoice's system IDs (student_id, user_id) never break.
5
Nothing measures the parent's experience. No stage timestamps, no owner per stuck case and no metric for paid but class missed. 16.7% of paid invoices have no form at all, so Ops doesn't know those children renewed.

What I propose

Let the payment drive

Gateway webhook → reconciliation engine. For same-batch renewals the teacher form and manual approval are deleted, not automated.

Humans handle only exceptions

18 reason codes, each with one owner, an SLA and a drafted message. Failed payments are never approved; partials and discounts go to Finance or the Ops Lead.

Never break a live class

A child keeps the current seat until the new term is booked. Shadow mode comes before switch-on, and a kill switch sends everything back to manual.
Prototype result on the official data: 8 of 12 open renewals auto-approved (67% straight-through), 192 class sessions generated ready to insert, 4 true exceptions routed to Finance, Ops Lead, Scheduler and Parent, and 22 WhatsApp/Slack messages drafted. Projected impact at ~400 renewals a week: ≈110 Ops hours a week saved (≈2.7 FTE), and same-batch payment-to-schedule time cut from days to under 15 minutes.

Failure-mode taxonomy (this sample plus what to expect at scale)

Trigger

Teacher forgets, is on leave, or quits mid-term. Weekend and late-night payments wait for Monday (2 of 13 paid on a Sunday).

Identity

A different payer phone or email, siblings under one account, NRI formats, homoglyphs, masked PII, re-used numbers.

Money

Partial or installment payments, failed or pending UPI, unapproved discounts, FX (US/GCC), refunds and chargebacks after scheduling.

Data entry

Wrong invoice ID or link, wrong amount, wrong batch, duplicates, free-text remarks that carry critical facts.

Scheduling

A false "batch full", changes approved without a slot, parents who never answer the slot picker, teacher leave clashes.

Visibility

No stage timestamps, no owner, no SLA clock, no "paid but missed class" metric. The problem is felt by parents before Ops sees it.

Design choices worth defending

  • Gateway is truth. Form amounts and screenshots never override it.
  • Match on IDs, not contacts. Phone and email differences are advisory only.
  • Fuzzy fallback only when the match is unique; otherwise it's an exception.
  • Homoglyph normalisation before any text comparison.
  • Seat-aware capacity: a renewing child never counts against their own seat.
  • Idempotent: re-running produces the same decisions, and duplicates are closed, not re-booked.
  • Works offline: no API key is needed to run or review it.

Deliverable D · SOP & change management

Rolling out without breaking a single live class

Prime directive: no existing student is ever removed from their current batch until the new term's sessions are written and confirmed. Every mode (manual, shadow, auto) obeys this. Class gaps are prevented by design, not by vigilance.

Change management for 200+ teachers

  • The pitch is "less work": "You no longer fill the renewal form." Removing a task needs little persuasion.
  • Pilot with 20 high-renewal teachers from different subjects and regions (India, GCC, US), including the ones with the most form errors.
  • Train in 10 minutes: a WhatsApp voice note, a 60-second screen recording and a pinned FAQ. No classroom training.
  • Feedback loop: teachers see their renewals confirmed in real time. The Teacher League recognises good performers and doesn't publicly name poor ones.

Change management for the 10-person Ops team

  • Role shift, not role cut: from "verifier" to "exception specialist". No redundancies in the first 90 days; freed time goes to parent experience and batch planning.
  • Two Ops champions co-own the rule book and sign off every new reason code.
  • Shadow mode builds trust: agents see the engine agree with them ≥98% of the time before it acts alone.
  • Kill switch: one flag sends everything back to the manual queue. Auto-approvals are reversible for 24h.
